## What Is an ATS Score?

An ATS (Applicant Tracking System) resume score is a 0–100% rating that measures how well
your resume matches a specific job description. It is calculated by comparing your resume's
keywords, job titles, skills, and format against requirements extracted from the job posting.

- **98%** of Fortune 500 companies use ATS for initial screening
- **75%** of resumes are rejected by ATS before a human reads them
- **Only 2–3%** of applicants pass ATS screening to reach the interview stage
- ATS-optimised resumes receive **3x more** human views
- Recruiters spend **6-7 seconds** on initial resume scan (The Ladders eye-tracking study)
- Only **1 in 10** resumes include measurable results (Resume Now, 18.4M resume analysis)
- Resumes with **15+ exact keyword matches** rank in the top quartile 73% of the time (Taleo data, 40,000+ submissions)
- Resumes with **<8 exact matches** rank in the bottom half 89% of the time

## ATS Score Thresholds — What They Mean

| Score Range | What It Means              | Likely Outcome                            | Action Required                              |
|-------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| 90–100%     | Near-perfect match         | Top-ranked. Very high callback probability | Verify accuracy. Prepare for interview.      |
| 80–89%      | Strong match               | Likely to reach recruiter                  | Polish phrasing. Check 2–3 remaining gaps.   |
| 75–79%      | Good — above threshold     | Passes most ATS filters                    | Add 2–4 high-priority missing keywords.      |
| 60–74%      | Borderline                 | Passes some ATS. Filtered by others.       | Significant keyword gap. Tailor urgently.    |
| 40–59%      | Below threshold            | Auto-filtered by most ATS platforms        | Add 10+ keywords. Rewrite summary.           |
| Below 40%   | Very low match             | Near-certain automatic rejection           | Full keyword and format audit required.      |

**Universal rule for 2026:**
- Minimum target: **75%**
- Standard roles: **80%+**
- Competitive / Fortune 500 roles: **85%+**
- Never submit below **60%**

**Competitive reality:** Your score is not just a pass/fail gate — it is a **ranking**. At a
high-volume role with 200+ applicants, even a 70% score may never reach a recruiter if 50
candidates scored 80%+. The goal is to **rank in the top 10–20** for that role.

## How ATS Systems Score Your Resume — 5 Stages

### Stage 1: Document Parsing
ATS converts your PDF/DOCX into raw text. Scanned images, locked PDFs, two-column
layouts, text boxes, and tables can all break parsing. Content that isn't parsed scores as absent.

### Stage 2: Entity Extraction
ATS maps extracted text to structured fields: job titles, companies, dates, education,
certifications, skills. Creative section headers like "My Journey" instead of "Work Experience"
cause fields to be missed or misclassified.

### Stage 3: JD Keyword Extraction
ATS builds a target keyword list from the job description including: required hard skills,
tools, certifications, job title, soft skills, industry terminology, and preferred qualifications.

### Stage 4: Matching Algorithm
Three methods are used (varies by platform):
- **Exact string matching** — "Python 3" must appear as "Python 3", not "Python programming"
- **Weighted scoring** — job titles and required certs carry more weight than preferred skills
- **Semantic/NLP matching** — advanced ATS can infer "people management" from "team leadership"
  (but don't rely on this — use exact JD language where possible)

### Stage 5: Ranking & Threshold Filtering
ATS ranks all applicants by score. Resumes below the threshold are auto-rejected.
Recruiters at high-volume companies may only review the **top 10–20 ranked resumes**.
A score of 70% may never be seen if 50 candidates scored 80%+.

## Keyword Strategy

### Keyword Tiering — Classify Before You Optimise

Before adding keywords, classify them into three tiers:

| Tier | What It Includes | Rule |
|------|-----------------|------|
| **Tier 1 — Hard Requirements** | Tools, technologies, certifications listed as mandatory/required | MUST appear in resume. Missing = hard filter on legacy ATS. |
| **Tier 2 — Repeated Competencies** | Soft skills, methodologies mentioned 2+ times in JD | Should appear where naturally applicable. |
| **Tier 3 — Industry & Culture** | Company-specific language, industry jargon, culture fit signals | Bonus points. Signals genuine interest and fit. |

### Where to Place Keywords (in order of ATS weight)

1. **Professional Summary** — highest-weighted section. Include top 5–8 Tier 1 keywords here.
2. **Skills Section** — ATS parsers check here first for technical signals. 15–25 skills.
3. **Experience Bullets** — keyword in context carries MORE weight than isolated skill list. A keyword paired with a metric and evidence scores higher than the same keyword listed alone. **70-80% of your keywords should be in this section**, integrated into achievement statements.
4. **PDF Metadata (pdfkeywords)** — indexed by some parsers; keep in sync with content.

### Keyword Context Quality

Not all keyword placements are equal. Modern ATS and AI-enhanced systems evaluate context:

| Placement | Quality | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Experience bullet + quantified result | **Strongest** | "Developed **Python** ETL pipeline, reducing processing time by **40%**" |
| Experience bullet, no metric | **Strong** | "Built data pipelines using **Python** and **SQL**" |
| Professional Summary | **Strong** | "Analytics professional proficient in **Python**, **SQL**, and **Tableau**" |
| Skills section only | **Moderate** | "Python, SQL, Tableau" (no proof of competency) |
| PDF metadata only | **Weak** | Hidden from human review, minimal ATS value |

**Key insight:** A keyword with contextual proof (metric + action) scores **significantly higher**
than the same keyword in an isolated list. Prioritise embedding keywords in achievement bullets.

### Keyword Density — The Numbers

| Metric | Target | Red Flag |
|--------|--------|----------|
| Keywords per resume | 15–25 unique JD keywords | <8 = likely bottom half; >40 = stuffing risk |
| Per-keyword frequency | 2–3 appearances across different sections | >4-5 appearances of same keyword |
| Overall density | 1–2% of total resume words | >3% triggers stuffing detection |
| Stuffing penalty | — | 25–40% score reduction on modern ATS |

**Formula:** For a 500-word resume, target 5–10 unique keywords × 2–3 appearances each = 10–30 keyword instances = 2–6% of words being keywords. Stay in the 1–3% range.

### Keyword Density Rules
- Top priority keywords: appear **2–3 times** across different sections
- Use exact phrases from JD, not synonyms, for hard skills and tools
- Avoid isolated keyword dumps — context matters in AI-enhanced ATS
- Never keyword stuff — modern ATS penalise artificially dense repetition
- Include both spelled-out form and abbreviation: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"
- White text keyword stuffing is **detected and penalised** — automatic rejection on modern platforms

### How to Extract Keywords from a JD
1. Read the full JD including responsibilities, requirements, and preferred qualifications
2. Classify every keyword into Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3
3. Note exact phrases used for responsibilities (e.g., "fraud hypothesis" not "anomaly analysis")
4. Identify experience requirements (years, domains)
5. Check for soft skills specifically mentioned (stakeholder management, cross-functional)
6. Count how many times each keyword appears in the JD — higher frequency = higher priority
7. Target: capture all Tier 1 keywords, 80%+ of Tier 2, and as many Tier 3 as fit naturally

## The 6-Second Recruiter Scan

Recruiters spend 6–7 seconds on initial resume scan (eye-tracking research). In that time,
they look at three things:

1. **Top third of the page** — name, headline, professional summary
2. **Bold text and numbers** — metrics that catch the eye
3. **Job titles and company names** — social proof and career trajectory

Your resume **passes** the 6-second test if a recruiter can immediately answer:
- What role does this person target? _(headline/summary)_
- How many years of relevant experience? _(summary, first line)_
- What are their top 2–3 achievements? _(bold metrics in summary or first role)_

Your resume **fails** the 6-second test if:
- The headline is generic ("Experienced Professional")
- The summary is a wall of text with no bold metrics
- The first achievement bullet is buried below a long skills section
- No quantified metrics are visible without reading deeply

**Only 1 in 10 resumes include measurable results.** Adding real metrics is still a
competitive advantage in 2026.

## AI-Content Detection — The Third Gate (2026)

> Sourced from: theinterviewguys.com, theaicareerlab.com, huntr.co, careerenlightenment.com — Updated June 2026

A resume now passes three gates, not two: the ATS keyword screen, the recruiter 6-second scan,
and AI-content detection.

- Workday, Greenhouse, and Lever shipped AI-content classifiers in late 2025. A flagged resume
  is rarely auto-rejected outright, but it gets routed to a lower-priority queue.
- **80%** of hiring managers say they can spot AI-generated content, and **49%** auto-dismiss
  resumes they suspect were written by AI (2026 hiring research).
- Detectors are calibrated against a small set of tics. Em dashes and overuse of "however"
  are two of the strongest signals.

### How to use AI without getting flagged

- AI tailors and sharpens. AI never invents. Bring your real facts, numbers, and stories.
- Let AI do ~70% (structure, first phrasing, keyword surfacing). Rewrite ~30% in your own voice.
- Never paste optimizer output verbatim. That exact text is what the detector flags.
- Read every line out loud. If you would not say it in an interview, rewrite it.
- Verify every number. If you cannot defend it in one sentence with context, cut it.

### AI-tell red flags — remove on sight

| Red flag | Fix |
|----------|-----|
| Em dashes (—) | Use commas or periods |
| "however", "moreover", "furthermore" | Cut or use a period |
| "spearheaded", "leveraged", "synergy", "delve", "showcasing" | Specific action verbs: built, reduced, closed, trained, launched |
| Openers like "results-driven", "seasoned", "passionate professional" | Lead with role, scope, or a metric |
| Oddly precise fake numbers like "37.2%" | Use clean, defensible figures |
| Every bullet in the identical structure | Vary openings and XYZ order |

### Voice workflow for this repo

Before compiling any resume, run the text through two skills, in order:

1. `.cursor/skills/humanizer/SKILL.md` — strip AI-writing tells.
2. `.cursor/skills/writing-style/SKILL.md` — spartan, direct, no em dashes, no filler.

## Bullet Point Quality — The XYZ and CAR Formulas

### XYZ Formula (Google format)
> "Accomplished **[X]** as measured by **[Y]**, by doing **[Z]**"

Example: "Boosted SaaS conversions **35%** through A/B testing (Optimizely), generating **$1.2M** annual revenue."

### CAR Formula (Challenge-Action-Result)
> "Faced **[Challenge]**, took **[Action]**, achieved **[Result]**"

Example: "Faced data quality issues across 20K+ records; built automated validation pipeline in Python, achieving **40% improvement** in accuracy."

### Bullet Quality Checklist
- [ ] 80%+ of bullets contain a quantified metric
- [ ] 4–6 bullets per role (not 8–10)
- [ ] Strong action verb starts each bullet (not "Responsible for")
- [ ] At least 1 Tier 1 keyword embedded per bullet
- [ ] Each bullet is 1–2 lines maximum
- [ ] Numbers are verifiable — if you can't explain the metric in 1–2 sentences, remove it

### Metric Categories (when you think you "don't have numbers")

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| **Volume** | records processed, reports generated, users served, tickets handled |
| **Time** | hours/week saved, cycle time reduced, time-to-resolution |
| **Money** | cost savings, revenue generated, budget managed |
| **Quality** | error rate reduced, accuracy improved, SLA met |
| **Growth** | adoption rate, conversion rate, retention improved |
| **Scale** | team size, regions supported, stakeholders served |

## Formatting Rules

### What ATS Can Parse Cleanly
- Single-column layout with left-aligned text
- Standard section headers: "Professional Summary", "Work Experience", "Skills", "Education"
- Standard fonts: Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, Helvetica (10–12pt)
- Simple bullet points (•)
- Consistent date formats (MM/YYYY or Month YYYY)
- Text-based PDF (created from Word/Google Docs, not scanned)

### What Kills Your ATS Score
| Problem                    | Why It Hurts                                           |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Two-column layout          | ATS reads columns in wrong order or merges them        |
| Tables for layout          | Content is read out of sequence or skipped             |
| Text boxes                 | Content inside is skipped entirely                     |
| Headers and footers        | Contact info placed there may be missed                |
| Images / graphics / charts | ATS cannot read visual elements                        |
| Scanned PDF                | ATS cannot extract any text                            |
| Creative section headers   | Parser fails to classify the section correctly         |
| Inconsistent date formats  | Employment timeline parsing fails                      |
| Skill ratings (stars/bars) | Visual rating elements are invisible to parsers        |
| LaTeX-compiled PDFs        | Some ATS parsers struggle with LaTeX encoding          |

## Section-by-Section Optimisation

### Contact Information
- Full name, phone, professional email, city/state, LinkedIn URL
- Location is critical — many ATS filter by geography
- No photos, no full address, no graphics

### Professional Summary (3–4 lines max)
- Lead with job title + years of experience + top domain
- Include 5–8 highest-priority Tier 1 keywords from JD
- Include 1–2 quantified achievements (bold the numbers)
- Rewrite this section for every application
- This is the most-read section by both ATS and humans — invest the most time here

### Skills Section
- 15–25 skills in a clean comma-separated or line-separated text list
- No star ratings, bars, or visual scoring — these are invisible to parsers
- Group by subcategory: Technical Tools, Core Competencies, Domain Knowledge
- Use exact JD terminology — not synonyms
- Include both abbreviation and full form: "SAP Analytics Cloud (SAC)"

### Work Experience
- Format: Job Title | Company | Location | Date Range
- Bullet format: Action Verb + Specific Keyword + Quantified Result
- Every bullet should contain at least 1 Tier 1 keyword from the JD
- Bold key metrics — they serve as visual anchors for the 6-second scan
- Use past tense for previous roles, present tense for current
- 4–6 bullets per role, not 8–10
- 70–80% of your JD keywords should appear in this section with contextual proof

### Education
- Degree, Field | University | Graduation Year | GPA (if 3.5+)
- Include certifications in a separate "Certifications" section
- List both abbreviation and full name for certs
- Missing a required certification = hard filter on many ATS platforms

## Common ATS Mistakes — And How to Fix Them

| Mistake                              | Fix                                                                 |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Same resume for every application    | Tailor summary + skills + 2–3 bullets per JD                       |
| Missing exact JD keyword             | Use the JD's exact phrase, not a synonym                           |
| Keywords only in skill list          | Re-embed top keywords in summary and experience bullets with proof |
| Experience years mismatch            | Frame tool/domain experience broadly in summary                    |
| Slash in job title (Sr. / Analyst)   | Use & or "and" — ATS may split on slash                           |
| Abbreviation without full form       | Use "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" format                      |
| No location in header                | Add city, state/country to contact line                            |
| Missing mandatory cert/tool          | Add as "(working knowledge)" if partially familiar                 |
| PDF from LaTeX with encoding issues  | Test parse quality on Jobscan before submitting                    |
| Overlong resume (3+ pages)           | Trim to 1–2 pages; remove pre-10-year experience if not relevant   |
| No quantified metrics in bullets     | Add numbers to 80%+ of bullets using metric categories above       |
| Keyword stuffing (>3% density)       | Remove redundant mentions, redistribute across sections            |
| Generic headline                     | Match headline to exact JD title + top 2–3 domain keywords         |
| Weak action verbs                    | Replace "Responsible for" / "Helped" with strong verbs (Led, Built, Drove, Achieved) |
