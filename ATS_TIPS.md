## What Is an ATS Score?

An ATS (Applicant Tracking System) resume score is a 0–100% rating that measures how well
your resume matches a specific job description. It is calculated by comparing your resume's
keywords, job titles, skills, and format against requirements extracted from the job posting.

- **98%** of Fortune 500 companies use ATS for initial screening
- **75%** of resumes are rejected by ATS before a human reads them
- **Only 2–3%** of applicants pass ATS screening to reach the interview stage
- ATS-optimised resumes receive **3x more** human views

## ATS Score Thresholds — What They Mean

| Score Range | What It Means              | Likely Outcome                            | Action Required                              |
|-------------|----------------------------|--------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| 90–100%     | Near-perfect match         | Top-ranked. Very high callback probability | Verify accuracy. Prepare for interview.      |
| 80–89%      | Strong match               | Likely to reach recruiter                  | Polish phrasing. Check 2–3 remaining gaps.   |
| 75–79%      | Good — above threshold     | Passes most ATS filters                    | Add 2–4 high-priority missing keywords.      |
| 60–74%      | Borderline                 | Passes some ATS. Filtered by others.       | Significant keyword gap. Tailor urgently.    |
| 40–59%      | Below threshold            | Auto-filtered by most ATS platforms        | Add 10+ keywords. Rewrite summary.           |
| Below 40%   | Very low match             | Near-certain automatic rejection           | Full keyword and format audit required.      |

**Universal rule for 2026:**
- Minimum target: **75%**
- Standard roles: **80%+**
- Competitive / Fortune 500 roles: **85%+**
- Never submit below **60%**

## How ATS Systems Score Your Resume — 5 Stages

### Stage 1: Document Parsing
ATS converts your PDF/DOCX into raw text. Scanned images, locked PDFs, two-column
layouts, text boxes, and tables can all break parsing. Content that isn't parsed scores as absent.

### Stage 2: Entity Extraction
ATS maps extracted text to structured fields: job titles, companies, dates, education,
certifications, skills. Creative section headers like "My Journey" instead of "Work Experience"
cause fields to be missed or misclassified.

### Stage 3: JD Keyword Extraction
ATS builds a target keyword list from the job description including: required hard skills,
tools, certifications, job title, soft skills, industry terminology, and preferred qualifications.

### Stage 4: Matching Algorithm
Three methods are used (varies by platform):
- **Exact string matching** — "Python 3" must appear as "Python 3", not "Python programming"
- **Weighted scoring** — job titles and required certs carry more weight than preferred skills
- **Semantic/NLP matching** — advanced ATS can infer "people management" from "team leadership"
  (but don't rely on this — use exact JD language where possible)

### Stage 5: Ranking & Threshold Filtering
ATS ranks all applicants by score. Resumes below the threshold are auto-rejected.
Recruiters at high-volume companies may only review the **top 10–20 ranked resumes**.
A score of 70% may never be seen if 50 candidates scored 80%+.

## Keyword Strategy

### Where to place keywords (in order of ATS weight)
1. **Professional Summary** — highest-weighted section. Include top 5–8 keywords here.
2. **Skills Section** — ATS parsers check here first for technical signals.
3. **Experience Bullets** — keyword in context carries more weight than isolated skill list.
4. **PDF Metadata (pdfkeywords)** — indexed by some parsers; keep in sync with content.

### Keyword density rules
- Top priority keywords: appear **2–3 times** across different sections
- Use exact phrases from JD, not synonyms, for hard skills and tools
- Avoid isolated keyword dumps — context matters in AI-enhanced ATS
- Never keyword stuff — modern ATS penalise artificially dense repetition
- Include both spelled-out form and abbreviation: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"

### How to extract keywords from a JD
1. Read the full JD including responsibilities, requirements, and preferred qualifications
2. Mark every tool, technology, methodology, and skill explicitly named
3. Note exact phrases used for responsibilities (e.g., "fraud hypothesis" not "anomaly analysis")
4. Identify experience requirements (years, domains)
5. Check for soft skills specifically mentioned (stakeholder management, cross-functional)
6. Prioritise: mandatory requirements > preferred skills > nice-to-have

## Formatting Rules

### What ATS can parse cleanly
- Single-column layout with left-aligned text
- Standard section headers: "Professional Summary", "Work Experience", "Skills", "Education"
- Standard fonts: Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, Helvetica (10–12pt)
- Simple bullet points (•)
- Consistent date formats (MM/YYYY or Month YYYY)
- Text-based PDF (created from Word/Google Docs, not scanned)

### What kills your ATS score
| Problem                    | Why It Hurts                                           |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Two-column layout          | ATS reads columns in wrong order or merges them        |
| Tables for layout          | Content is read out of sequence or skipped             |
| Text boxes                 | Content inside is skipped entirely                     |
| Headers and footers        | Contact info placed there may be missed                |
| Images / graphics / charts | ATS cannot read visual elements                        |
| Scanned PDF                | ATS cannot extract any text                            |
| Creative section headers   | Parser fails to classify the section correctly         |
| Inconsistent date formats  | Employment timeline parsing fails                      |
| Skill ratings (stars/bars) | Visual rating elements are invisible to parsers        |
| LaTeX-compiled PDFs        | Some ATS parsers struggle with LaTeX encoding          |

## Section-by-Section Optimisation

### Contact Information
- Full name, phone, professional email, city/state, LinkedIn URL
- Location is critical — many ATS filter by geography
- No photos, no full address, no graphics

### Professional Summary (3–4 lines max)
- Lead with job title + years of experience + top domain
- Include 5–8 highest-priority keywords from JD
- Include 1–2 quantified achievements
- Rewrite this section for every application

### Skills Section
- 15–25 skills in a clean comma-separated or line-separated text list
- No star ratings, bars, or visual scoring — these are invisible to parsers
- Group by subcategory: Technical Tools, Core Competencies, Domain Knowledge
- Use exact JD terminology — not synonyms

### Work Experience
- Format: Job Title | Company | Location | Date Range
- Bullet format: Action Verb + Specific Keyword + Quantified Result
- Every bullet should contain at least 1 keyword from the JD
- Bold key metrics
- Use past tense for previous roles, present tense for current

### Education
- Degree, Field | University | Graduation Year | GPA (if 3.5+)
- Include certifications in a separate "Certifications" section
- List both abbreviation and full name for certs

## Common ATS Mistakes — And How to Fix Them

| Mistake                              | Fix                                                                 |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Same resume for every application    | Tailor summary + skills + 2–3 bullets per JD                       |
| Missing exact JD keyword             | Use the JD's exact phrase, not a synonym                           |
| Experience years mismatch            | Frame tool/domain experience broadly in summary                    |
| Slash in job title (Sr. / Analyst)   | Use & or "and" — ATS may split on slash                           |
| Keywords only in skill list           | Re-embed top keywords in summary and experience bullets            |
| Abbreviation without full form       | Use "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" format                      |
| No location in header                | Add city, state/country to contact line                            |
| Missing mandatory cert/tool          | Add as "(working knowledge)" if partially familiar                 |
| PDF from LaTeX with encoding issues  | Test parse quality on Jobscan before submitting                    |
| Overlong resume (3+ pages)           | Trim to 1–2 pages; remove pre-10-year experience if not relevant   |

## Pre-Submission Checklist

- [ ] Resume tailored to this specific JD (not generic)
- [ ] Professional Summary rewritten with JD keywords
- [ ] All mandatory skills from JD present in resume
- [ ] Job title in summary matches or is close to JD title
- [ ] Location present in contact header
- [ ] Single-column layout, standard section headers
- [ ] Text-based PDF (not scanned, not image-based)
- [ ] Standard font (Arial, Calibri, etc.), 10–12pt
- [ ] No tables, text boxes, graphics, headers/footers
- [ ] Date formats consistent throughout
- [ ] All metrics bolded or clearly stated
- [ ] 1-page (for <5 yrs experience) or 2-page max
- [ ] Run through Jobscan or Resume Worded — score 75%+
- [ ] Spell check and grammar check complete

## Key Insight: ATS is Competitive, Not Just Pass/Fail

Your score is not just a pass/fail gate — it is a **ranking**. At a high-volume role with
200+ applicants, even a 70% score may never reach a recruiter if 50 candidates scored 85%.

The goal is not just to pass the threshold — it is to **rank in the top 10–20** for that role.
